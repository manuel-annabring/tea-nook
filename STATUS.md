- Slice 2.1, what I did in Blender and why:

Replacing mesh data and leaving the object alone is what keeps its name, parent links, applied transforms and origin, so the contract holds.

- Slice 2.2, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Kettle body, lid and knob: I rebuilt the mesh data of the existing `Kettle` object in place with `bmesh`, revolving two (radius, height) profiles around Z (a "lathe"). The body is a truncated cone with a soft foot and rounded shoulder; the lid sits 4.5 mm proud with a low dome and a small knob. Each face gets a material index, so body, spout and handle use slot 0 (`Mat_Kettle`) and lid and knob slot 1 (`Mat_KettleDetail`), which keeps the two-mesh export group.
  - Spout: a hollow cone built as rings placed along an axis tilted up and towards -X; its base ring is buried in the wall and the tip ring is a thin rim around a real bore. Building rings perpendicular to the axis (a "sweep") gives an opening that really faces up and out.
  - Handle: a thick octagonal ring swept along a half ellipse on +X, like the mug handle but bigger, with both ends inside the wall so it reads as one piece. Normals are recalculated and faces are flat shaded.
  - Marker: `Steam_Kettle` is an Empty parented to `Kettle`, so I set only its local position to the centre of the spout opening; its rotation and scale stay at identity.

- Slice 2.3, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Rebuilt `Room` in place: `04_room.py` builds the whole room in one `bmesh` and writes it into the existing mesh data, so the object, its origin, applied transforms and the seven material slots stay. It is one merged mesh, as the contract requires, and `UVMap` is regenerated by the same box projection (a UV per face from its dominant axis) used in the blockout.
  - Window: frame bars, a cross mullion (four panes), a sill that sticks out into the room, a head board and side casing, all `Mat_Frame` boxes placed by parameters. `Window_Glass` and `Window_Sky` were not touched; the opening size and position are unchanged.
  - Cabinet and worktop: a recessed toe kick under the carcass, doors with a raised panel and a gap between them (a loop over both doors), vertical handles on two posts. The worktop box gets a bevel (a chamfer, one segment) on its front, right and corner edge via `bmesh.ops.bevel`; the footprint and top at z = 0 stay.
  - Floor and walls: boards are a loop of long boxes with 4 mm gaps, two alternating heights and a staggered joint per column. Baseboards and a top trim are thin boxes; the wall boxes end 14 mm lower so the trim brings the silhouette back to 0.50.
  - Plant, tin, books: pot and tin are a lathe (a profile of radius and height revolved around Z); leaves are a loop that places a folded 4-triangle diamond with a golden-angle yaw and two pitch tiers (an array-style fan). Books are cover, cover, spine and a page block in `Mat_Frame` each. Leaves are open shells with explicit winding, so they are skipped by the normals recalculation.
  - Markers: `Rays_Origin` follows `Window_Glass`; `Rays_Target`, `Dust_Min`, `Dust_Max`, `Orbit_Target` are re-set to the same values (still valid, checked against the new geometry).

- Slice 2.4, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Second UV layer: I added `UV_AO` next to `UVMap` on the `Room` mesh and unwrapped it with Smart UV Project, then Pack Islands. A UV layer is a second set of 2D texture coordinates per face corner; the first one (`UVMap`) stays as it was, the second is the one the AO picture is mapped with. An island is a connected patch of faces laid flat; for baking, islands must not overlap, or two faces would share (and overwrite) the same pixels, so the script rasterises every face and checks that.
  - Baking: I switched the render engine to Cycles for a moment and baked the Ambient Occlusion type, which shoots rays from every texel of `Room` and records how much of the hemisphere above it is blocked. The bake target is an image that an Image Texture node (set as the active node) points at in each Room material, and the active UV layer decides where texels land, so `UV_AO` was made active for the bake. The image is Non-Color data, because AO is a measurement, not a colour, and the Cycles margin setting smears island edges outwards so the gaps do not turn into dark seams. `Mug`, `Kettle` and `TeaSurface` stay in the scene so they block rays and the contact shadow under them lands on the worktop; the window glass and sky are hidden for the bake so they do not black out the corner.
  - Clean-up and levels: all temporary nodes, the image, the world and the engine switch are removed or restored, so the materials are exactly as before. The result is blurred a little, levelled so the darkest value is 40 % and written as an 8 bit PNG.
- Slice 2.4, what the baked texture shows (`public/models/room_ao.png`): mostly white, because most faces are open to the room. Floor, wall and worktop islands are white with soft grey falling off into corners and under overhangs; the worktop island has four dark footprints where the tin, mug, kettle and books stand; the island of the back wall behind the cabinet is grey where the cabinet blocks it.
