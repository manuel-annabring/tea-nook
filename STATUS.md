## Gotchas

- The site is served under `/tea-nook/` (also in dev). `http://localhost:5173/` alone is not the app.
- The built-in browser pane defaults to 800 x 600. Use `resize_window` to 1280 x 720 for screenshots. A screenshot taken right after navigation can show only the background: wait for the ready flag (slice 1.1).
- The browser pane renders pages only while awake; the `data-nook-ready` flag may stay unset until a screenshot or other pane action. Poll again, then screenshot.
- The Leva dev panel covers the right third of the view; hide it with `?panel=0` (slice 1.1).
- `.claude/` is git-ignored, so permission settings and `launch.json` are local only.
- There is no git remote. Do not push.
- Typewriter's Blender tooling to adapt lives in `~/Projects/typewriter/tools/` (outside this repo, read only).
- The Blender add-on server must be started by Manuel for phase 2 only. Phases 1, 3, 4 and 5 use headless `Blender -b`.
- Plan is Pro (5-hour window, weekly limit, extra usage off). Check `get_usage` before each slice.

- Slice 2.1, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Mug body: I rebuilt the mesh data of the existing `Mug` object in place with `bmesh` (one revolved profile for outer wall, rounded lip, inner wall and floor). Replacing mesh data and leaving the object alone is what keeps its name, parent links, applied transforms and origin, so the contract holds.
  - Handle: swept a small 8-sided ring along a half ellipse (a D loop) and joined it to the body mesh; its ends sit inside the wall so it reads as one piece. Normals are recalculated so they point outwards and faces are flat shaded, like the blockout.
  - Tea and marker: `TeaSurface` is a 20-gon disc built at local z 0 (origin at its centre) and lifted by moving only the object, 8 mm under the rim. `Steam_Tea` is an Empty, so only its position matters; it now sits at the opening centre at rim height.

- Slice 2.2, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Kettle body, lid and knob: I rebuilt the mesh data of the existing `Kettle` object in place with `bmesh`, revolving two (radius, height) profiles around Z (a "lathe"). The body is a truncated cone with a soft foot and rounded shoulder; the lid sits 4.5 mm proud with a low dome and a small knob. Each face gets a material index, so body, spout and handle use slot 0 (`Mat_Kettle`) and lid and knob slot 1 (`Mat_KettleDetail`), which keeps the two-mesh export group.
  - Spout: a hollow cone built as rings placed along an axis tilted up and towards -X; its base ring is buried in the wall and the tip ring is a thin rim around a real bore. Building rings perpendicular to the axis (a "sweep") gives an opening that really faces up and out.
  - Handle: a thick octagonal ring swept along a half ellipse on +X, like the mug handle but bigger, with both ends inside the wall so it reads as one piece. Normals are recalculated and faces are flat shaded.
  - Marker: `Steam_Kettle` is an Empty parented to `Kettle`, so I set only its local position to the centre of the spout opening; its rotation and scale stay at identity.

- Slice 2.3, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Rebuilt `Room` in place: `04_room.py` builds the whole room in one `bmesh` and writes it into the existing mesh data, so the object, its origin, applied transforms and the seven material slots stay. It is one merged mesh, as the contract requires, and `UVMap` is regenerated by the same box projection (a UV per face from its dominant axis) used in the blockout.
  - Window: frame bars, a cross mullion (four panes), a sill that sticks out into the room, a head board and side casing, all `Mat_Frame` boxes placed by parameters. `Window_Glass` and `Window_Sky` were not touched; the opening size and position are unchanged.
  - Cabinet and worktop: a recessed toe kick under the carcass, doors with a raised panel and a gap between them (a loop over both doors), vertical handles on two posts. The worktop box gets a bevel (a chamfer, one segment) on its front, right and corner edge via `bmesh.ops.bevel`; the footprint and top at z = 0 stay.
  - Floor and walls: boards are a loop of long boxes with 4 mm gaps, two alternating heights and a staggered joint per column. Baseboards and a top trim are thin boxes; the wall boxes end 14 mm lower so the trim brings the silhouette back to 0.50.
  - Plant, tin, books: pot and tin are a lathe (a profile of radius and height revolved around Z); leaves are a loop that places a folded 4-triangle diamond with a golden-angle yaw and two pitch tiers (an array-style fan). Books are cover, cover, spine and a page block in `Mat_Frame` each. Leaves are open shells with explicit winding, so they are skipped by the normals recalculation.
  - Markers: `Rays_Origin` follows `Window_Glass`; `Rays_Target`, `Dust_Min`, `Dust_Max`, `Orbit_Target` are re-set to the same values (still valid, checked against the new geometry).

- Slice 2.4, what I did in Blender and why:
  - Second UV layer: I added `UV_AO` next to `UVMap` on the `Room` mesh and unwrapped it with Smart UV Project, then Pack Islands. A UV layer is a second set of 2D texture coordinates per face corner; the first one (`UVMap`) stays as it was, the second is the one the AO picture is mapped with. An island is a connected patch of faces laid flat; for baking, islands must not overlap, or two faces would share (and overwrite) the same pixels, so the script rasterises every face and checks that.
  - Baking: I switched the render engine to Cycles for a moment and baked the Ambient Occlusion type, which shoots rays from every texel of `Room` and records how much of the hemisphere above it is blocked. The bake target is an image that an Image Texture node (set as the active node) points at in each Room material, and the active UV layer decides where texels land, so `UV_AO` was made active for the bake. The image is Non-Color data, because AO is a measurement, not a colour, and the Cycles margin setting smears island edges outwards so the gaps do not turn into dark seams. `Mug`, `Kettle` and `TeaSurface` stay in the scene so they block rays and the contact shadow under them lands on the worktop; the window glass and sky are hidden for the bake so they do not black out the corner.
  - Clean-up and levels: all temporary nodes, the image, the world and the engine switch are removed or restored, so the materials are exactly as before. The result is blurred a little, levelled so the darkest value is 40 % and written as an 8 bit PNG.
- Slice 2.4, what the baked texture shows (`public/models/room_ao.png`): mostly white, because most faces are open to the room. Floor, wall and worktop islands are white with soft grey falling off into corners and under overhangs; the worktop island has four dark footprints where the tin, mug, kettle and books stand; the island of the back wall behind the cabinet is grey where the cabinet blocks it. - F4 Blender notes (what and why, for learning). (1) Floor plant: the pot is a lathe (a profile of radius and height points, spun around Z) with only 8 sides, which gives the flat octagonal facets Manuel wanted; the stems are 4-sided prisms along a gently bowed path (a tube is just rings of vertices joined by quads); each leaf is a single open surface of 10 triangles. Single surfaces work because every material is double sided in the GLB, so a leaf costs 10 triangles instead of 24 for a thin closed shell. (2) Wall plant: same building blocks (lathe bowl, tubes for ropes and vines, 6-triangle hexagon "coins" with a slightly raised middle), plus a box bracket; the script checks that nothing leaves its allowed area and fails loudly otherwise. (3) Floor joints: in the AO bake each face gets its own piece of the texture (a UV island); a 4 mm board edge got one pixel, so its shading was just a blur of its neighbours. Scaling those islands up before packing gives them a few real pixels, so the bake can darken the crack, and the colour of the lit side face no longer shows. The vertical side faces of the boards are lit more brightly than the tops by the hemisphere light (ground colour is brighter than sky colour), which is why only a real AO value fixes it.
- F4 weak points: the groove line is fairly dark (about -30 % at its darkest pixel); if Manuel wants it softer, raise `FLOOR`-style limits for those islands or lower `BOOST_MAX` in `05_ao_bake.py`. The wall plant covers the lower left corner of the window frame on screen at the hero view (in 3D it is 10 cm in front of the wall and clear of the sill); its vines pass in front of the tin and the sill edge. The wall plant is not in the sun path (it hangs outside the lit tube behind the window), so it casts no visible sun shadow; it only gets contact AO. Leaves are flat folded shapes (low poly on purpose), they read like paper at close zoom.

- G2 Blender notes (what and why, for learning). (1) Smooth shading per face: every face has a "smooth" flag; flat faces use one normal per face, smooth faces blend the normals of the faces around each vertex. `Room` is one mesh with many parts, so `04_room.py` collects the plant faces while it builds them and sets only their flag (the leaf is a separate shell, so it blends only with itself); a tube end cap gets sharp edges so the stem ends stay crisp. The glTF exporter writes these as vertex normals, and the vertex count drops because smooth neighbours share vertices (leaf triangles 90 with 99 vertices instead of 270). (2) Wavy vines: each vine is a polyline whose points get two sine offsets (one radial, one sideways) with a different phase and amplitude, faded in with a smoothstep so the vine leaves the rim straight; the leaves are rotated with `Quaternion.rotation_difference` (the rotation that turns "down" into the local tangent), blended 70 %. (3) Irregular leaves: a seeded `random.Random` gives each leaf vertex a radius jitter and each leaf an oval aspect, so a re-run gives exactly the same mesh. (4) Floor joints: the AO texture is multiplied only onto the hemisphere light; direct lights (sun, front fill) ignore it, so a face the front fill hits head-on can never be darkened by baking. We hide such faces with geometry (the lower far piece behind the higher near piece) and fix the bake so the faces that can be darkened really are. (5) `07_window_lower.py`: moving a window quad is just changing its object location (the origin is at its centre, rotation and scale stay applied); the script sets an absolute height from the original constants, so a second run changes nothing, and it refuses to save if `Room`'s window opening is not at the same height.
- G2 pipeline order after any change to `04_room.py`: `04_room.py -- --force`, then `07_window_lower.py` (only needed if the window moved), then `05_ao_bake.py`, contract check, export, `npm run gen:model`.
